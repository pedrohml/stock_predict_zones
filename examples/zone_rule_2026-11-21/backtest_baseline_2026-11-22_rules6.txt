  1806 case(s) scored
  Zone rule (choose_zones): width = 1.5 times the mean of the two typical_zone_width numbers of
    `rz.py levels`. Candidates are the prices `levels` reports as evidence: pivot lows and highs,
    the lows and highs of the last three candles, the retracement levels and the two ends of the
    dominant swing; those below the price serve supports, those above it resistances. Walking
    outward from the price, a candidate within one width of the first member of the current cluster
    joins it; a cluster of fewer than two candidates is dropped. Each remaining cluster's level is
    the mean of its members, and its zone is one width centred there, cut at the price and pushed
    outward so it never overlaps the zone before it. The nearest clusters are kept. A side with too
    few clusters is filled with one-width zones, one width apart, beyond the last one.
  This is the unadjusted baseline (rz.py sim) on mechanically chosen zones: not the zones or the
    adjusted probabilities of a real projection, and no events, dividends or earnings shocks.
  Cases overlap whenever the step is shorter than the horizon, and the entries of one case share one
    price path, so the forecasts are far from independent; n overstates the evidence.
  Tickers chosen today are survivors. Prices are split-adjusted to today's scale and not dividend-
    adjusted, so a large dividend shows up as a drop the simulation did not include.
  Calibration compares probabilities with events. It is not a measure of returns and does not show
    that the method works or fails.

  Zone rule (choose_zones): width = 1.5 times the mean of the two typical_zone_width numbers of
    `rz.py levels`. Candidates are the prices `levels` reports as evidence: pivot lows and highs,
    the lows and highs of the last three candles, the retracement levels and the two ends of the
    dominant swing; those below the price serve supports, those above it resistances. Walking
    outward from the price, a candidate within one width of the first member of the current cluster
    joins it; a cluster of fewer than two candidates is dropped. Each remaining cluster's level is
    the mean of its members, and its zone is one width centred there, cut at the price and pushed
    outward so it never overlaps the zone before it. The nearest clusters are kept. A side with too
    few clusters is filled with one-width zones, one width apart, beyond the last one.
  This is the unadjusted baseline (rz.py sim) on mechanically chosen zones: not the zones or the
    adjusted probabilities of a real projection, and no events, dividends or earnings shocks.
  Cases overlap whenever the step is shorter than the horizon, and the entries of one case share one
    price path, so the forecasts are far from independent; n overstates the evidence.
  Tickers chosen today are survivors. Prices are split-adjusted to today's scale and not dividend-
    adjusted, so a large dividend shows up as a drop the simulation did not include.
  Calibration compares probabilities with events. It is not a measure of returns and does not show
    that the method works or fails.

  Zone rule (choose_zones): width = 1.5 times the mean of the two typical_zone_width numbers of
    `rz.py levels`. Candidates are the prices `levels` reports as evidence: pivot lows and highs,
    the lows and highs of the last three candles, the retracement levels and the two ends of the
    dominant swing; those below the price serve supports, those above it resistances. Walking
    outward from the price, a candidate within one width of the first member of the current cluster
    joins it; a cluster of fewer than two candidates is dropped. Each remaining cluster's level is
    the mean of its members, and its zone is one width centred there, cut at the price and pushed
    outward so it never overlaps the zone before it. The nearest clusters are kept. A side with too
    few clusters is filled with one-width zones, one width apart, beyond the last one.
  This is the unadjusted baseline (rz.py sim) on mechanically chosen zones: not the zones or the
    adjusted probabilities of a real projection, and no events, dividends or earnings shocks.
  Cases overlap whenever the step is shorter than the horizon, and the entries of one case share one
    price path, so the forecasts are far from independent; n overstates the evidence.
  Tickers chosen today are survivors. Prices are split-adjusted to today's scale and not dividend-
    adjusted, so a large dividend shows up as a drop the simulation did not include.
  Calibration compares probabilities with events. It is not a measure of returns and does not show
    that the method works or fails.
